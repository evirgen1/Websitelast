## Course Description

This class explores scholarship from comparative politics, international relations, and political sociology that examines the causes, dynamics, and consequences of democratic backsliding and state repression. As we connect history, theory, and scholarship to contemporary politics, key questions include: Why do democracies erode, and what role do institutions, leaders, and citizens play in that process? How and why do governments employ repression, and with what consequences for political opposition and civil society? How do different regime types (democratic, authoritarian, and hybrid) shape the nature and extent of state repression?

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will have a good understanding of the major theoretical debates about democratic decline and authoritarian entrenchment, along with knowledge of how these processes have unfolded in a range of countries such as the United States, Hungary, Poland, Turkey, Venezuela, Russia, and beyond.

## Topics

1. Introductions and Political Regimes
2. Authoritarian Regimes
3. Democracy
4. Hybrid Regimes
5. Democratic Backsliding
6. What is State Repression?
7. Why States Repress?
8. Repression Types (I): Political violence, disappearances, and judicial and extrajudicial methods
9. Repression Types (II): Political imprisonment, torture, and online crackdowns
10. Repression Types (III): Propaganda and censorship
11. Justification of State Repression
12. State Repression in Different Political Regimes (I)
13. State Repression in Different Political Regimes (II)
14. Responses to State Repression
